Privacy

What this site collects, which is almost nothing

Looking at photographs

Browsing collects nothing about you. There are no analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels and no third-party scripts. Nothing is contacted on your behalf — not even Google — until you choose to react to a picture.

Reactions

Pressing an emoji asks you to sign in with Google. This exists for one reason: so each person counts once rather than a single visitor being able to press a button a hundred times.

When you react, this site stores your name, your email address, your Google account identifier, the target you reacted to, the emoji, and the time. That is the complete list.

Other visitors never see any of it. The public pages show counts only — that four people reacted, not who they were. The stored details are visible only to me, the person who runs the site. (Comments are the one exception, and they show initials rather than names — see below.)

To remove a reaction, tap it again. That deletes the record, not just the count. Reactions are also deleted automatically after a year without change.

Comments

Commenting also requires a Google sign-in, and stores the same details as a reaction plus the text you wrote.

A comment is shown publicly with your initials only — “Stephen Young” appears as “SY”. Your full name and email are not published; they remain visible only to me.

You can delete your own comments at any time using the delete link on them, which removes the record entirely.

Signing in

Sign-in uses Google's standard flow. This site never sees your password. Google gives it a signed token containing your name, email address and account id, which is verified and then discarded — the token itself is never stored. In exchange this site issues a session that lasts 90 days so you are not asked again on every visit.

You can revoke this site's access at any time from your Google account permissions page. Revoking stops future sign-ins; to delete reactions you have already left, tap them again or email me.

View counts

Each target page shows how many times it has been viewed. To avoid counting the same person repeatedly, a one-way hash of your IP address is kept for 24 hours and then deleted. Your IP address itself is never written down, and the hash cannot be turned back into one. No login is needed or asked for.

Where it lives

The site is static pages hosted by GitHub Pages. Reactions and view counts are handled by a small program running on Cloudflare Workers, storing data in Cloudflare's key–value store. Both companies will see request logs in the ordinary course of serving the site.

Changes and contact

This is a personal astrophotography site run by one person as a hobby. Nothing here is sold, shared or used for advertising, and there is no mailing list. To ask what is stored about you, or to have it deleted, email stephenyoung7267@gmail.com.
