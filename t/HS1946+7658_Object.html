HS1946+7658 Object

Three pixels, and they are the oldest thing in this entire archive.

HS 1946+7658 is a quasar in Draco at redshift 3.05. Its light left when the universe was 2.1 billion years old — about 15 percent of its present age — and has been in transit for 11.7 billion years. That is more than twice the age of the Earth. This light was already most of the way here before the Sun existed.

What is actually producing it is gas spiralling into a supermassive black hole and heating to millions of degrees on the way down. When it was found in the Hamburg-Schmidt survey it was briefly the most luminous object known to astronomy.

There is nothing to process. No structure, no nebulosity, no colour to balance. Just a magnitude 15.9 dot among a few thousand other dots, marked here because otherwise you would never pick it out. The dot next to it, slightly brighter and just above, is an ordinary star in our own galaxy — a few hundred light years away instead of eleven billion.

Ten minutes of light. Five 120-second frames on a 76 mm refractor, no filter, from a back yard in southern New Hampshire. Detected at signal-to-noise 7.6, and confirmed by plate solving to within 2.1 arcseconds of its catalogued position.

The photons that made this image are older than the Earth, older than the Sun, and older than most of the elements they passed through on the way.
